  1. The vendor lists the food

    A photo, what it is, the price and a pickup window, in under a minute on a phone. The window must be at least 30 minutes long, end at least 60 minutes after publishing, and end at least 30 minutes before the food's safe-until time.

    8:30 pm

  2. Verified NGOs nearby see it

    Listings are shown only to approved organisations within their search area, sorted by the pickup that ends soonest. Organisations that have set alerts get an email.

    From 8:30 pm

  3. An NGO claims it

    The first organisation to tap Claim gets the food, and it is held for them while they pay. The vendor gets a WhatsApp message.

    9:12 pm

  4. The NGO pays

    By Raast, or by JazzCash or Easypaisa wallet, through PayFast. Once the payment is confirmed, the NGO sees the pickup code, the vendor's address and phone number, and the vendor gets a second WhatsApp message to get the food ready.

    By 9:42 pm, the payment deadline

  5. The collector picks it up

    The NGO's driver checks the food, then gives the 4-digit code at the counter. The vendor types it in and confirms the quantity handed over.

    Between 10:00 and 10:45 pm

  6. The NGO confirms

    The NGO is asked "Did you receive everything?" and can report a problem with photos. If it doesn't reply within 24 hours, the order is Completed.

    Within 24 hours of pickup

  7. The vendor is paid

    Once the order is Completed and the payment has settled, the vendor's 95% goes out in the next Monday or Thursday payout, by bank transfer to the business's own account.

    Within 6 working days of pickup

During the January 2027 pilot, NGOs will pay the vendor directly, to the vendor's own Raast QR or account, before the same deadline, and then tap "I've paid". The vendor confirms "Payment received" within 15 minutes, which issues the pickup code. If they don't, the food stays held and our team phones both sides. No commission is charged during the pilot.

{% include section-head.html eyebrow="Pickup codes" title="A 4-digit code proves the right people met" id="codes-title" lead="Food is handed over only when the vendor types in the code the NGO received after paying. That one step closes the order." %}
  • The code appears for the NGO as soon as its payment is confirmed, with the vendor's address, a map link and phone number. It stays readable on the phone if the signal is weak.
  • The NGO sends a collector pass to its driver by WhatsApp. The driver needs no account: the pass shows the code, the address and the pickup time.
  • At the counter, the driver checks the food's look, smell and temperature first. If it looks unsafe, they tap "Refuse food" on the pass and add a photo, and the NGO is refunded in full.
  • If the food is fine, the driver gives the code. The vendor or their counter staff type it in, see the NGO, the collector's name and "Paid", confirm the quantity, and tap Confirm handover.
  • The vendor never sees the code in advance. They can only check a code that is typed in, so food can't be handed to the wrong person.

The commission is 5% of the listing price, with services tax included, rounded down to the whole rupee. The vendor's 95% is before any tax the law requires us to withhold, which is shown line by line on their statement.

Rizq Rasan pays the payment gateway's fees out of its 5% on normal sales. There's no sign-up fee and no monthly charge.

How fast we act

Either side can report a problem, add photos and ask for a full or partial refund. Every case gets a reference number, and both sides see the decision in writing.

Strikes expire after 60 days. One strike brings a warning, two bring a 48-hour freeze on new claims or listings, and three bring a 7-day suspension and a review by our team. We can forgive one strike every 90 days on appeal.
