From the start of the pilot

  • Tandoor breads: naan, roti, paratha, sheermal
  • Plain bakery: bread, buns, rusk, plain cake
  • Mithai and desserts without cream
  • Fruit and vegetables
  • Packaged and dry goods: atta, rice, daal, oil, biscuits
  • Drinks and juices
{% include icon.html name="clock" size=22 %}

From 1 February 2027

Cooked food opens 4 weeks after the pilot starts, but only if there has been no food incident in that time:

  • Cooked rice: biryani, pulao
  • Curries, daal, haleem, nihari, salan
  • Fried snacks and iftar items: samosa, pakora, rolls
{% include icon.html name="thermometer" size=22 %}

Later, in Phase 2

Foods that need a strict cold chain open after launch:

  • Bakery with cream, custard or filling
  • Dairy: milk, dahi, paneer, lassi

Before publishing, every vendor confirms two statements: "This food was never served to customers and is not a plate return" and "This food has been stored correctly and is safe to eat."

Not allowed on Rizq Rasan

  • Food past its use-by date
  • Home-cooked food
  • Plate returns or part-eaten food
  • Opened dairy drinks
  • Recalled products
  • Alcohol and tobacco
  • Raw meat, poultry and fish, for now
  • Frozen food, except packaged goods up to their label date

When the clock starts

Cooked food, bakery and mithai count from the time they were cooked or baked. Fruit and vegetables count from when the listing is published. Packaged goods, dairy and drinks follow the use-by or best-before date on the pack.

Cooked food left at room temperature gets at most 2 hours, counted from when it was set out. This follows the 2-hour rule of Food Standards Australia New Zealand for food between 5°C and 60°C. Hot-held food is capped at 4 hours as our own quality limit.

Mixed trays

A tray with more than one kind of food takes the strictest limit of anything in it. The vendor ticks which categories the tray contains.

These limits follow the WHO Five Keys to Safer Food and the FSANZ 2-hour and 4-hour rule. They will be confirmed with a food-safety adviser or the Islamabad Food Authority before launch.

{% include icon.html name="thermometer" size=20 %}

Hot months rule. From May to September, cooked food can't be listed at room temperature at all. It must be hot-held at 60°C or more, or chilled to 5°C or less, because 2 hours at 40°C is not safe.

{% include icon.html name="shield" size=22 %}

Before you list

  • Hold a valid Islamabad Food Authority licence. We check it before you can list.
  • List only food that was never served to customers.
  • Pick the right category, so the right time limit applies.
  • Tell us within 24 hours about any food incident or action by the Islamabad Food Authority.
{% include icon.html name="camera" size=22 %}

Describe it honestly

  • Add 1 to 3 real photos of the food. The first photo is required.
  • Give the quantity and an estimated weight.
  • Enter when it was cooked or baked, and copy the use-by or best-before date from the pack for packaged goods and drinks.
  • Mark allergens and whether it contains beef, mutton, chicken or fish, so NGOs can serve it safely. Halal shows as "vendor-declared" unless you upload a halal certificate.
{% include icon.html name="thermometer" size=22 %}

Store and pack it safely

  • Keep chilled food at 5°C or below and hot-held food at 60°C or above until handover.
  • Say how it's packed: sealed containers, foil trays, or loose, in which case the NGO brings its own containers.
  • Cooked food stored chilled must have been chilled within 2 hours of cooking.
{% include icon.html name="clock" size=22 %}

Set a safe pickup window

  • The window must be at least 30 minutes long.
  • Pickup must end at least 30 minutes before the food's safe-until time.
  • Pickup must end at least 60 minutes after you publish, so NGOs have time to claim, pay and travel.
  • Hand food over only when the collector gives the right 4-digit code, and let them check it first.
{% include section-head.html eyebrow="For NGOs" title="Rules for NGOs" id="ngos-title" lead="Once the food is handed over, the NGO is responsible for it. These rules apply to every pickup." %}
{% include icon.html name="alert" size=22 %}

Check it at pickup

  • Look at the food, smell it and check its temperature before the pickup code is entered.
  • If anything seems unsafe, don't take it. Tap "Refuse food" and add a photo. You're refunded in full straight away.
  • If the listing says the food is loose, bring your own clean containers.
{% include icon.html name="truck" size=22 %}

Carry it safely

  • Take the food straight to your kitchen or distribution point.
  • Keep chilled food cold and hot food hot on the way.
{% include icon.html name="clock" size=22 %}

Serve it in time

  • Serve or refrigerate cooked food as soon as it arrives.
  • Never serve food after its safe-until time.
  • If you cook or reheat food, you may need your own Islamabad Food Authority licence. We'll confirm this before launch.
{% include icon.html name="heart" size=22 %}

Give it, never sell it

  • Food claimed through Rizq Rasan is for the people you serve. Reselling it means permanent removal.
  • Keep a simple log of where the food went.
  • Report any problem with the food within 24 hours of pickup.

NGO Inspecting the food at pickup and refusing it if it's unsafe. Carrying it safely. Serving it within its time limit. Keeping a simple distribution log. Never reselling it.

How complaints and disputes work {% include icon.html name="arrow-right" size=18 %}

  1. Get help first

    If someone may be ill from the food, get medical help before anything else.

    Straight away

  2. Report it

    On the platform, use "Report a problem" on the order, with what went wrong, up to 3 photos and how much you received. Or email us.

    Within 24 hours of pickup

  3. We act on it

    With one action, our team can freeze every live listing from that vendor in the same category and warn every NGO that collected food from the same batch. We keep the photos and timeline and cooperate with the Islamabad Food Authority.

    Acknowledged within 4 working hours

  4. We decide

    Both sides are heard, and the decision goes to both in writing. An upheld complaint means a full or partial refund for the NGO and a strike for the vendor.

    Decided within 24 hours
